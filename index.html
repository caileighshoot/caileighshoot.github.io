About

I'm a data scientist who spends most of my time turning messy sensor data — LiDAR point clouds, hyperspectral and multispectral imagery, UAV captures — into models, dashboards, and tools that people actually use to make decisions. Over the past decade that's meant everything from benchmarking classification algorithms for a thesis to shipping an internal tool that saved a Fortune 500 company over a million dollars.

My path into data science started in Yosemite. As an undergraduate researcher I worked a season as a field research assistant in the park, then built my capstone project under Dr. L. Monika Moskal and Dr. Van Kane: using airborne LiDAR and machine learning to detect low-stature understory vegetation hidden beneath Yosemite's sugar pine and mixed-conifer canopy. It won the University of Washington's ESRM Senior Capstone Award — and it's what convinced me that the most interesting questions are the ones you can only answer by modeling data the human eye can't fully interpret on its own.

I carried that into my Master of Science in the Remote Sensing and Geospatial Analysis Laboratory at the University of Washington, completed in June 2018. My thesis fused hyperspectral imaging and LiDAR to classify forest type in the Tanana Valley of interior Alaska, benchmarking five machine learning algorithms — co-chaired by Dr. Moskal and Dr. Hans-Erik Andersen, in collaboration with NASA scientists, and published in Remote Sensing in May 2021.

Since 2018 I've worked in remote sensing and applied data science for the largest forestry company in the US, moving between inventory, research, and carbon teams. As a Remote Sensing Research Specialist I integrated LiDAR, multispectral imaging, and especially UAVs into Weyerhaeuser's research program, logging well over 150 flights — many of them solo. In my most recent role there, as Lead Forest Carbon Scientist, I built automated decision-support tools in R Shiny, Power BI, and ArcGIS — one of which delivered more than $1.3M in cost savings and won the company's 2025 Innovator of the Year award.

In summer 2022, with Weyerhaeuser's support, I took a part-time position as a Remote Sensing lecturer at the University of Washington, teaching 115 students for an academic quarter. I loved it, and the students seemed to too — the course closed with a 4.1/5 evaluation score.

I'm now a PhD researcher at TU Berlin, applying remote sensing and machine learning to understand forest recovery after large-scale disturbances — bark beetle outbreaks, windthrow, wildfire — while looking for my next role as a data scientist ready to bring that same rigor to a new domain.

Featured project

MS Thesis Code · R

Classifying FIA Forest Type from a Fusion of Hyperspectral & LiDAR Data

A full processing and modeling pipeline built for my Master's thesis: fusing NASA G-LiHT hyperspectral and LiDAR data to classify forest type across the Tanana Inventory Unit in interior Alaska. I tested five classification algorithms — Naive Bayes, k-NN, multinomial logistic regression, SVM, and Random Forest — across six combinations of spectral, topographic, and canopy-height inputs, validated with 3-fold cross-validation.

The best model — Random Forest using hyperspectral vegetation indices plus topography and canopy height — reached 77.53% overall accuracy. A follow-on analysis applied the same pipeline to detect aspen leaf miner damage across interior Alaska, reaching 83% accuracy identifying aspen presence.
