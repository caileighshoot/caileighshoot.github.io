About

I specialize in using remote sensing and GIS to solve problems and answer questions about earth's natural resources. My background includes work with LiDAR, hyperspectral and multispectral imaging, and drones/UAVs. I started my career in remote sensing as an undergraduate researcher in September 2014, during which time I worked on my capstone under Dr. L. Monika Moskal and Dr. Van Kane. For this project I determined that LiDAR can be used to detect understory presence in the sugar pine/mixed-conifer forests of Yosemite National Park. During this time, I was also able to work as a field research assistant in Yosemite.

In June 2018 I completed my Master of Science in the Remote Sensing and Geospatial Analysis Laboratory at the University of Washington. My MS thesis work involved using hyperspectral imaging and LiDAR to classify forest type in the Tanana Valley of interior Alaska, and was co-chaired by Dr. L. Monika Moskal and Dr. Hans-Erik Andersen. After graduating, I was hired as an intern at NASA Goddard Space Flight Center, where I was able to finish processing my thesis on NASA's supercomputer. This work was published in Remote Sensing in May 2021.

Read My Thesis Publication

Since 2018 I have worked in forest remote sensing and GIS for the largest forestry company in the US. In that time I've worked as a Remote Sensing Research Specialist, an Inventory and Planning GIS Analyst, and most recently as Lead Forest Carbon Scientist. As a Remote Sensing Research Specialist, I worked to integrate remote sensing into Weyerhaeuser's research projects, including the use of LiDAR, multispectral imaging, and especially UAVs. While in this role, I logged well over 150 UAV flights, many of which were flown solo. I handled all the flight planning, equipment maintenance, and data processing myself.

As an Inventory and Planning GIS Analyst, I performed GIS mapping and spatial analysis in direct support of Weyerhaeuser's operational forestry teams, and provided user support and training for both Esri products (ArcGIS Desktop, Field Maps, Survey123, and more) and Weyerhaeuser's internal forest management systems and databases.

In the summer of 2022 I was offered a part-time position as a Remote Sensing lecturer at the University of Washington, filling in for a faculty member. With Weyerhaeuser's support, I gladly accepted and taught 115 students remote sensing part-time, while also working my full-time job at Weyerhaeuser, for an academic quarter. I very much enjoyed teaching and was grateful for the opportunity. By the end of the quarter, I'd earned strong course evaluations: 4.1 out of 5 overall, with 71% of students responding.

As Lead Forest Carbon Scientist, I used data analysis (in Python and R), GIS, and remote sensing to lead the development of high-integrity forest carbon projects. Much of my focus was on building tools and documentation to make carbon projects easier to execute. That included one automated decision-support tool that delivered over $1.3M in cost savings and won Weyerhaeuser's 2025 Innovator of the Year award. I'm now a PhD researcher at TU Berlin, applying remote sensing and machine learning to understand forest recovery after large-scale disturbances like bark beetle outbreaks, windthrow, and wildfire. I'm also looking for my next role as a data scientist, ready to bring that same rigor to a new domain.

MS Thesis Code · R

Classifying FIA Forest Type from Hyperspectral & LiDAR Data

Fused NASA G-LiHT hyperspectral and LiDAR data to classify forest type in interior Alaska, benchmarking five machine learning algorithms. The best model, a Random Forest using hyperspectral vegetation indices, topography, and canopy height, reached 77.53% overall accuracy.
